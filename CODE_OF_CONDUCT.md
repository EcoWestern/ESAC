# Code of conduct

ESAC accepts no code contributions, but it has a specification, a set of suites, and issue
trackers, and those are read by people. The expectations below apply to everyone
participating there, maintainers included.

## Expected behaviour

- Be direct about technical substance and civil about the person. The two are independent.
- Engage the claim, not the author. "This clause can be read two ways, here is the second
  reading" is useful. "This specification is worthless" is not wrong because it is harsh, but
  because it gives nothing to work with.
- Assume a report is made in good faith until there is evidence otherwise.
- Respect that a change may be declined. A closed issue is a design decision, not a personal
  verdict.
- Keep reports free of API keys, held-out seeds, and private evaluation material.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discriminatory comments about a person's identity or background.
- Publishing anyone's private information.
- Deliberately derailing or hijacking issue threads.
- Sustained disruption, including spam, repeated re-litigation of closed issues, and pressure
  campaigns aimed at individual maintainers.
- Using a benchmark result, or the absence of one, as a pretext to attack a person or a team.

## Scope

This applies in all program spaces: issue trackers, any repository operated by the project,
and any place where an individual is representing the project in public.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a concern privately through the channel described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
Reports are read by the maintainers only.

Maintainers may warn, edit or delete comments, lock threads, or block accounts. Consequences
escalate with severity and repetition. Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this code in
good faith may face consequences determined by the other maintainers.
